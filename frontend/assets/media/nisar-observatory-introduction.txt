A changing planet. A clearer view. Meet NISAR Observatory: an interactive workspace that turns satellite radar measurements into maps of surface change.

Start in Explore Earth. Select a region, find available observations, and download only the area you need. Use a guided tool, or build an editable sequence of processing steps.

Area and analysis settings keep the geographic boundaries, measurement choices and quality controls together, with explanations to guide each decision.

Tools explains seven focused methods, from water and fire changes to ground and glacier motion. Saved personal workflows appear in My Tools.

Examples provides prepared case studies with real input files, tested settings and comparisons with published research or independent evidence.

Mission library explains the spacecraft, radar products and how to interpret them. This application uses NASA and ISRO mission data from the L-band radar, accessed through NASA archives.

Runs keeps completed analyses and their source records, ready to reopen.

Connection manages the Earthdata session for remote downloads. Prepared local examples can run without signing in.

About introduces Carmelo Sammarco and the project’s NASA Space Apps challenge: Dancing with the SARs.

Now consider a simple question: where did water appear around Chokwe, Mozambique? This example uses observations from January fifth and January seventeenth, twenty twenty-six. Its prepared inputs and settings make the analysis repeatable.

Radar measures reflected signals, rather than taking an ordinary photograph. The workflow screens unreliable samples, then applies a documented rule to identify dark returns consistent with open water.

Blue marks places newly meeting that water-candidate rule. This setup identifies about ninety point five square kilometres of new candidates. It shows where to investigate; it does not measure flood depth or damage.

NASA’s published views show a similar expansion across the floodplain. Their wider perspective and different visualization prevent a pixel-for-pixel accuracy claim. With only two dates, the example also cannot establish when the water receded.

Results can be reopened, exported as illustrated maps or numeric geographic data, and adapted into reusable workflows. Keep the settings and limitations with every result.

NISAR Observatory brings the data, processing steps and explanations into one place, making surface change easier to explore and communicate. Choose a place. Follow the evidence. Make the change understandable.
